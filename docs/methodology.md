# Methodology

Nic Structured Learning treats learning as a change in what a learner can independently do, not merely what they recognise when an explanation is visible.

## 1. Structure before detail

Technical learners can perform isolated procedures without understanding when or why to use them. The skill therefore begins substantial topics with a compact map of:

- the problem being solved;
- prerequisite knowledge;
- the central concepts and their relationships;
- the procedure or mechanism;
- the result and its applications.

The map is used as orientation, not as a replacement for worked reasoning.

## 2. Connection before memorisation

New ideas are attached to existing knowledge. For example, partial derivatives are introduced only after checking ordinary derivative rules; eigenvectors are connected to matrix transformations before the characteristic equation is introduced.

When a learner is stuck, the tutor should identify the earliest missing dependency instead of repeating the entire solution at greater length.

## 3. Application before assessment memorisation

Definitions become useful when the learner can apply them. The skill moves from meaning to procedure and then to a small application before compressing material for revision.

Assessment guidance must remain evidence-based. A topic is described as assessed only when supported by course or assessment materials.

## 4. Adaptive support

Support should change with demonstrated performance:

1. Explain or model the missing reasoning step.
2. Ask the learner to reproduce the same method without notes.
3. Change the numbers, representation, or context.
4. Ask the learner to transfer the method.

If the learner fails, diagnose the exact point of failure: terminology, prerequisite knowledge, method selection, algebra, calculation, interpretation, or answer format.

The tutor then repairs that point and retests it. It does not assume that seeing the correction produces mastery.

## 5. Mastery evidence

### Recognition

The learner follows or agrees with a visible solution. This is useful feedback, but not evidence of independent performance.

### Reproduction

The learner solves the same type of problem without notes. This is the minimum evidence that the procedure has been learned.

### Transfer

The learner solves a changed version independently. The changed problem may use different numbers, notation, representation, context, or required reasoning. This is the target for assessment readiness.

## 6. Responsible source use

Course materials are authoritative for course-specific notation, scope, emphasis, and assessment requirements. Explanatory background may be added, but should not be attributed to the course unless supported by the supplied materials.

The tutor should explicitly identify uncertainty or contradictions instead of quietly inventing an answer.

## 7. Intended evaluation

The skill can be evaluated with paired learning tasks:

- a guided example;
- a same-type closed-notes reproduction problem;
- a changed transfer problem;
- a brief learner explanation of the selected method.

Useful evaluation questions include:

- Did the tutor identify the correct prerequisite gap?
- Did guidance decrease after successful performance?
- Did the transfer task change something meaningful?
- Did feedback identify the reasoning error rather than only the wrong result?
- Were course-specific claims traceable to supplied materials?
